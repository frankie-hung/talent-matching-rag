# Product Manager

**Team:** Merchant Experience, Starferry Payments (fictional)  
**Type:** Permanent, hybrid — Sheung Wan  
**Reports to:** Director of Product

## About the role

Starferry Payments provides checkout and settlement tools for small and mid-sized merchants in Hong Kong and nearby markets. We need a product manager for the merchant web app: onboarding, payout visibility, and the screens a finance clerk uses when a settlement looks wrong.

This is not a project-coordinator seat and not a purely technical specification seat. You will decide what is worth building, write it so engineering can challenge it, and stay with the metric after release.

## What you will do

- Run discovery with merchants, support leads, and internal ops. Separate “a loud ticket” from “a repeated job to be done”.
- Own a quarterly roadmap. Say no, and write down why, when sales asks for a one-off bank format.
- Specify problems, non-goals, and acceptance criteria. Include edge cases: partial refunds, multi-currency payouts, and holiday cut-offs.
- Work with design and engineering in a two-week cadence. You join refinement; you do not throw a deck over the wall.
- Instrument funnels. After launch, report activation, time-to-first-payout, and support contacts per active merchant.
- Align compliance and finance early when a change touches how money moves or how we explain fees.

## What you bring

- At least four years as a product manager, product owner with real scope, or equivalent in a B2B software company.
- A shipped example where you killed or narrowed a feature after talking to users.
- Comfort with payments, fintech, or other operational B2B software. You do not need to be an engineer; you do need to read a sequence diagram.
- Clear writing in English. Stakeholder updates are short and specific.

## Nice to have

- Hong Kong merchant, SME, or acquiring exposure.
- Experience with support tooling and what makes a ticket defensible.
- Familiarity with experimentation on low-traffic B2B funnels, where you cannot wait for a huge sample.
- Cantonese for merchant interviews. English remains the working language for specs.

## How we work

Sheung Wan hybrid, with merchant visits in Kowloon and occasional trips to Singapore. We write decisions in the open. Roadmap reviews are working sessions, not theatre.
